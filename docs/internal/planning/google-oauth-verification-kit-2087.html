Google Calendar verification (#2087)

Your steps, in order: set up, record, submit. Built 10-23 from PPM’s kit (docs/internal/planning/google-oauth-verification-kit-2087.md), with what we learned from your outside-account connect this morning.

Why this matters: partners now see “Google hasn’t verified this app” before they can connect Calendar. The name and logo were verified on 10-22. This submission is the other half: Google’s review of the read-only calendar access. Once Google approves it, the warnings go away.

Where: the Google Cloud project Piper Morgan (Alpha), number 43539582958. Not the plain “Piper Morgan” project.

Your time: about 10 minutes of setup, a 2-minute recording, then the form. Google’s review then takes days to weeks.

Stage 1 Declare the scope (console, before recording)

  1. Open console.cloud.google.com and use the project picker at the top to choose Piper Morgan (Alpha).
  2. Google Auth Platform → Data Access → Add or remove scopes. Tick .../auth/calendar.readonly and .../auth/userinfo.email (search “calendar.readonly” if it isn’t listed). Then Update, then Save. If a justification box appears beside the sensitive scope, paste the text from Stage 4.
  3. Branding: check it reads app name Piper Morgan (Alpha), homepage https://pipermorgan.ai, privacy https://pipermorgan.ai/privacy/, authorized domain pipermorgan.ai. Leave Terms blank (there’s no Terms page, by design). Change nothing here.
  4. Audience: confirm External and In production.

Stage 2 Set up for a clean recording

  1. Calendar disconnected in Piper: on alpha, Settings → Integrations → Google Calendar reads not connected. Your Gmail’s Linked apps already reads empty, so Google has forgotten the grant.
  2. Test events for today on that Gmail calendar. Your bongo lessons, practice and recital are perfect.
  3. Hide what reviewers shouldn’t see: bookmarks bar off (Cmd-Shift-B in Chrome), other tabs closed, Do Not Disturb on, so no notifications pop up.
  4. Browser in English, window wide enough that the address bar stays visible the whole time. Reviewers match the URLs.
  5. Two tabs ready: pipermorgan.ai, and alpha.pipermorgan.ai signed in to the test account you used this morning.
  6. Recorder ready: Cmd-Shift-5 → record the browser window (or QuickTime → New Screen Recording).

Stage 3 Record (one take, about 2 minutes)

  1. pipermorgan.ai: scroll briefly to show what Piper does, then click Privacy in the footer and let the privacy page show.
  2. alpha.pipermorgan.ai → Settings → Integrations. Show Google Calendar not connected, then press Connect.
  3. Choose the Gmail account.
  4. “Google hasn’t verified this app”: show it, click Advanced, then Go to Piper Morgan (Alpha) (unsafe). Expected; reviewers want to see it.
  5. Sign in to Piper Morgan (Alpha): Continue.
  6. The consent page: tick the calendar box (“See and download any calendar you can access…”), then hold still about 5 seconds so the app name, logo and calendar line are all readable. Then Continue.
    Stop and redo if Google skips straight past the consent page. That means it still remembers the old grant. Remove Piper at myaccount.google.com/permissions and start again.
  7. Back in Piper: Calendar reads Connected. Press Test and let “Test Passed” show.
  8. Chat: ask “What’s on my calendar today?” and let the reply list the bongo events.
    Ask about today, not “this week”. This week’s calendar has real meetings with other people’s names, and the video goes to Google’s reviewers and sits on YouTube.
  9. Settings → Integrations → Google Calendar → Disconnect. This shows a user can revoke, and it ends Piper’s access. Stop recording.

Stage 4 Upload and submit

  1. Upload to YouTube as Unlisted. Copy the link.
  2. Console → Verification Center → Prepare for verification / Submit, in Piper Morgan (Alpha).
  3. Scope justification (the Data Access box “How will the scopes be used?” and the form): paste this. It’s 862 of the 1,000 characters allowed, and it includes the narrower-scope answer.
    Piper Morgan is a product-management assistant. When a user asks about their schedule (for example "what's on my calendar today?") or starts their daily standup, Piper reads that user's own calendar events for the period asked and answers in the chat. Piper only reads: it never creates, changes or deletes events. We request calendar.readonly rather than calendar.events.readonly because Piper also reads the user's calendar list to find their primary calendar and its time zone, which events.readonly does not cover. Event details are used only to answer the user who connected the calendar; they are sent to the AI provider that user chose, with their own key, to write that answer. We never sell the data, use it for advertising, or use it to train AI models. The access token is stored encrypted, and disconnecting revokes it at Google and deletes our copy.
  4. If asked “why not calendar.events.readonly?”
    Piper also reads the user's calendar list to find which calendar is primary and its time zone. calendar.readonly covers that; calendar.events.readonly doesn't.
  5. Video link from YouTube, and a contact email of your choice. That stays out of the repo.
  6. Submit. Then tell me it’s in. Google mails the developer contact with any questions, and if it asks for changes, forward the gist and PPM keeps #2087 current.
