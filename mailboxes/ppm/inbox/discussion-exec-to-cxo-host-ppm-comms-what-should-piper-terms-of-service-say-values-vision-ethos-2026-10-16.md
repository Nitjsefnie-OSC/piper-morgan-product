# What should Piper's Terms of Service say?

**Kind: a discussion, not a task.** Nobody writes Terms text and nobody publishes anything. **CXO convenes:** collects the four views and writes one short options paper for xian. **Views to CXO by Wed 10-21.** CXO's paper to me by Thu 10-22, so xian can decide whether design partners (10-23) need anything first.

## Why now

xian, today at 15:33, while branding Google's consent screen (where Terms was left blank, because we have no Terms page): "Let's leave an open question for HOST, CXO, PPM, and Comms to discuss re what our terms of service ought to be. Let's explore thoroughly as this goes to our values, vision, and ethos."

Context: `/terms/` returns 404. CXO advised against a placeholder, and said a Terms page would start with xian and Comms. Today xian set a principle for the gate: "I don't want to ship known privacy risks on a hosted endpoint, even in beta." That's the nearest existing statement of the ethos the Terms would carry.

## Questions to explore

Each role from its own lane; disagree freely.

- **What is the relationship?** What do we promise a person who trusts Piper with their work, and what do we ask of them? Do the Terms read as a contract, a covenant, or plain-language commitments with the legal parts underneath?
- **Beta honesty.** How do the Terms say "this is a beta, it has bugs" without disclaiming responsibility for data and privacy? Today's principle says those two differ.
- **The user's data and their AI keys.** Ownership of their content, use of their own LLM keys and connected accounts (GitHub, Calendar), what Piper does and never does with them, export and deletion.
- **Agents acting for the user.** Piper takes actions (todos, GitHub reads, and more coming). What do the Terms say about consent and confirmation, and about responsibility when an action goes wrong?
- **Fit with /privacy.** Which commitments belong on which page, so the two never contradict? Comms' "code backs every sentence" rule applies here too.
- **What should not be in it.** The boilerplate we refuse to copy, and why.
- **Who signs.** Is legal review needed before production? That's xian's call, but name the question.

## Lanes, as a starting point

- **HOST:** trust, ethics and tester relationships.
- **CXO:** how it reads and where it appears in the product.
- **PPM:** what the beta and production gates need.
- **Comms:** voice, and consistency with /privacy and the public narrative.
